The Unverifiable Model: Trust, Optimisation and the Economics of LLM Inference

By Konrad Strachan • Anera Labs Research • 12 min read

Open-source artificial intelligence has rapidly caught up in raw capability with centralized proprietary models. However, the bottleneck to global deployment is no longer algorithmic—it is financial and economic.

1. The Core Dilemma of Unverifiable Compute

Unlike deterministic software execution, large language model (LLM) inference outputs vary across quantization levels, hardware architecture, and kernel optimizations. Without cryptographically verifiable compute indices, users face an information asymmetry: how do you verify that an inference cloud delivered full 16-bit precision versus quantized 4-bit output?

"Inference pricing cannot mature into a commodity market until price discovery is decoupled from individual cloud provider trust boundaries."

2. Financializing Inference Margins

Anera Labs builds index benchmarks (`anera.markets`) and non-dilutive working capital structures (`anera.finance`) that transform GPU capacity into standardized, tradable forward instruments. This allows GPU clusters to lock in revenue and scale infrastructure sustainably.
